## The short version

Be decent. Argue about the work, not the person.

## What is expected

Technical disagreement is welcome and is most of what happens here. Take it to
the evidence: measure the thing, show the output, name the file and the line.
"This is wrong because the capture shows X" is a contribution. "This is wrong
because you do not know what you are doing" is not.

Assume the person you are replying to is competent and has a reason. Most of the
defects recorded in this repository were made by careful people, and several
were made by whoever is reading this.

Say when you are unsure. An honest "I have not verified this" is worth more here
than confidence, and the project's own rules say so at length.

## What is not acceptable

Personal attacks, harassment, and dismissing somebody on the basis of who they
are rather than what they wrote. Publishing anybody's private information.
Sustained disruption of discussion.

## A specific one, for this project

Do not ask contributors or users to identify themselves, name their country, or
describe the network conditions they are operating under. This is
censorship-circumvention software. For some of the people using it and working
on it, that information is dangerous, and a question asked casually can put
somebody at risk who then feels unable to refuse it.

If a bug report is missing detail you need, ask for the technical detail
directly. What the software did is the question. Where the reporter was standing
is not.

## Reporting

Through the contact on the project's own website. Reports are read by the
maintainer, and are not made public.

## Enforcement

The maintainer may remove comments, close pull requests, and block accounts.
There is no process here more elaborate than that, and pretending otherwise
would be dishonest for a project this size.
